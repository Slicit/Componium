# Contributor License Agreement

`icla.txt` is the agreement a contributor signs before their first pull request
is merged.

## Why this exists

Componium is AGPL-3.0 and is also offered under separate commercial terms. That
second option only remains possible while one party holds sufficient rights
over the whole codebase.

Merge one contribution with no agreement in place and the project becomes
jointly owned by everyone who has ever contributed. Commercial licensing then
requires tracking down every one of them for permission, which in practice
means it is over. That is not reversible.

## What it is

The **Apache Individual Contributor License Agreement v2.2**, adapted only by
changing the receiving party from the Apache Software Foundation to this
project. Nothing else was rewritten.

That choice is deliberate. Bespoke legal text written for a hobby project is a
liability: contributors have to read it carefully because it is unfamiliar, and
nobody has checked it. The Apache ICLA is one of the most widely signed
agreements in open source, most contributors have signed one before, and its
section 2 grant includes the sublicensing right that dual licensing depends on.

The original is at <https://apache.org/licenses/icla.pdf>. The text here was
extracted from that PDF, not retyped.

## What a contributor grants

In plain terms, and the agreement governs where this summary and it differ:

- You keep the copyright in your contribution. Nothing is assigned away.
- You grant a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable copyright licence **including
  the right to sublicense**, which is what permits commercial relicensing.
- You grant an equivalent patent licence covering your contribution.
- You confirm you are entitled to make the contribution: it is your own work,
  and where an employer holds rights to what you write, you have permission.

## For companies

Contributors acting on behalf of an employer need the Corporate CLA rather than
this one. It is at <https://apache.org/licenses/cla-corporate.pdf> and has not
been adapted here yet, because nobody has needed it.

## Not legal advice

This document and the adaptation were prepared by a programmer, not a lawyer.
**Have it reviewed before relying on it to sell a commercial licence.** The
cost of getting it wrong is discovering years later that the commercial option
was never available, at which point nothing can be done about it.
